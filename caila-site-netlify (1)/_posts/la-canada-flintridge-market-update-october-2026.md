Last month I finally gave La Cañada its own deep dive. This month, I'm glad that I did. While the market story tends to be synonymous between these sister areas, in October the stories were on opposite ends of the bookshelf. While [La Crescenta's October numbers](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-crescenta-market-update-october-2026) tightened, La Cañada's ended up looser than they were a year ago, and that contrast is the story this month.

Let's take a look at La Cañada Flintridge's market data: October 2025 compared to October 2026, plus the trailing twelve months of MLS data, for single family homes and the small condo segment. If you missed [last month's La Cañada update](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-canada-flintridge-market-update-september-2026), it's worth a skim for the comparison.

A quick note on the data: these numbers come from MLS reports I pulled on November 6, 2026. MLS counts get revised as late-reported sales come in, so every comparison below uses the current figures.

**La Cañada Flintridge's single family market is looser than it was a year ago, the opposite of La Crescenta: months of supply doubled from two to four, active listings rose 21 percent, and pending sales fell 34 percent from a year ago, although compared with September the market held steady, and the trailing-year numbers show prices holding near a $2.41 million median.**

That's the headline. Here's what's underneath it.

## October Looked Like a Different Market Than La Crescenta

Closed single family sales came in at 15 in October 2026, down from 20 in October 2025, a 25 percent drop. Pending sales fell harder, to 19 from 29, a 34 percent decline. Active listings rose to 69 from 57, up 21 percent, and months of supply (how long the current inventory would take to sell at the recent pace) doubled to 4 from 2. Four months is close to balanced, a noticeable step away from the seller-favored two months of a year ago.

The supply side explains part of this. New listings jumped to 21 from 13, up 62 percent. More homes came to market, fewer went under contract, and the inventory piled up. Over in La Crescenta, new listings fell 44 percent over the same stretch.

La Cañada is a smaller market than La Crescenta, with 158 closed sales over the past year versus 231, so single months swing harder in percentage terms. One soft October isn't a new trend by itself, but it does stand out against how La Crescenta's supply moved this month.

Compared with September, though, October was steadier than the year-over-year picture suggests. Active listings barely moved (67 to 69), months of supply held at 4, new listings held steady, and both pending sales (16 to 19) and closed sales (11 to 15) ticked up. The year-over-year gap is partly about how tight October 2025 was, with only 2 months of supply, so my read is that this is a market that has settled at a looser level, not one that kept loosening in October.

## Prices Fell on Paper, but Buyers Weren't Getting Discounts

The October numbers look like a big price drop at first glance. The average sale price was $2,435,859 versus $2,770,550 a year ago, down 12 percent, and the median was $2,105,000 versus $2,403,750, also down about 12 percent.

Before you sellers panic (or you buyers assume La Cañada has an end of year clearance going on), two things. First, there were only 15 closings. With a sample that small, a handful of sales at lower price points can move the monthly average and median a lot. Second, the homes that did sell weren't selling at a discount: the average sale price came in at 105 percent of the last list price in October, versus 101 percent a year ago. The homes that did sell were still closing at or above asking, on average.

The trailing-year numbers, below, tell a steadier story than any single month.

Days on market held up too. The average days on market was 24 versus 27 a year ago, and the median was 11 days versus 14. Fewer homes sold, but the ones that did moved quickly.

## The Trailing Year: Prices Holding

The twelve-month rollup now covers November 2025 through October 2026.

158 single family homes closed over the trailing year, for a combined $452,842,256 in total sold volume. The average sale price came in at $2,866,090 and the median at $2,412,500. Homes took 27 days to sell on average, sale prices averaged 101.84 percent of list prices, and average price per square foot landed at $1,009.68.

Against last month's rollup, the closed count dipped from 163 to 158, which is mostly the window rolling forward: October 2025's 20 closings dropped out and October 2026's 15 came in. The median edged down from $2,445,090 to $2,412,500, the average from $2,893,959 to $2,866,090, and average days to sell stayed at 27. I read that as a small step back, not a trend.

The biggest price band was $2,000,001 to $3,000,000, with 64 of the 158 sales, about 41 percent. Another 43 sold between $1 million and $2 million, and 50 sold above $3 million, including 10 above $5 million.

Speed remains strong. 119 of the 158 sales, about three-quarters, closed within 30 days of hitting the market, a higher share than La Crescenta's two-thirds. Only 5 sat longer than 120 days.

Compared with [La Crescenta](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-crescenta-market-update-october-2026), the trailing-year median sale price here was about 72 percent higher ($2,412,500 versus $1,400,000), and average price per square foot was about 22 percent higher ($1,009.68 versus $826.58). A year of data shows a real and consistent premium, without a big difference in how fast homes sell.

## Condos: Still a Market That Barely Exists Here

La Cañada's condo segment is small enough that month-to-month moves don't mean much. Active condo listings were 4 in October 2026, up from 2 in September and 2 a year ago. One condo closed in October, and there were none in October 2025.

Over the trailing twelve months, only 10 condos closed, for a combined $13,132,000. The average sale price was $1,313,200 and the median was $1,399,500. Condos took 44 days to sell on average, sale prices averaged 104.44 percent of list prices, and price per square foot averaged $739.43. Eight of the 10 sold between $1,250,000 and $1,568,000, and two sold for under $1 million. Six closed within 30 days, while two sat longer than 120. Compared with last month's rollup, the count went from 9 closings to 10 (October's one sale), the median held at about $1.4 million, and average days to sell improved to 44 from 46.

With ten sales to look at, treat those averages as a snapshot, not a trend. The comparison that matters: La Crescenta had 53 condo closings over the same period at a median of $800,000, so the median condo price in La Cañada ran about 75 percent higher. If condo selection matters to your search, [La Crescenta's update](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-crescenta-market-update-october-2026) covers that segment in more detail.

## What This Means If You're Buying

October gave La Cañada buyers more of what they've been missing: more homes to choose from (69 active versus 57) and less competition per listing (pending sales down about a third). That's a better setup for taking your time and being selective. But it isn't a discount window. The homes that sold went for 105 percent of list price on average, and the twelve-month median barely moved.

With more choice, the terms of your offer matter more, including which [contingencies](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/what-are-real-estate-contingencies) you keep. Two practical things before you're seriously touring: get [pre-approved, not just pre-qualified](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/pre-approval-vs-pre-qualification), and budget for [what homeowners insurance costs here now](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/foothills-homeowners-insurance-after-eaton-fire), since it's a bigger line item than it used to be.

## What This Means If You're Selling

More listings and fewer pending sales mean more competition for the buyers who are out there. October's numbers don't say homes stopped selling: pending and closed sales both rose from September, and the ones that closed averaged 105 percent of list price and a median of 11 days on market. They do say that accurate pricing and good presentation matter more when buyers have a longer list to choose from. Getting your [seller disclosures](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/california-seller-disclosures-tds-spq-avid-nhd) organized before you list helps keep things moving once an offer arrives.

### Is La Cañada Flintridge a buyer's or seller's market right now?

Based on October 2026 MLS data, La Cañada Flintridge single family homes are closer to balanced, with months of supply rising from 2 a year ago to 4, where it also sat in September. Anything under three months is generally considered a seller's market, and four months is close to balanced. Homes that sold still averaged about 105 percent of list price in October.

### Did home prices fall in La Cañada Flintridge in October?

On paper, yes. The October median sale price was $2,105,000 versus $2,403,750 a year earlier, down about 12 percent, but that is based on only 15 closings. The trailing twelve-month median was $2,412,500 and the average was $2,866,090.

### How much more expensive is La Cañada Flintridge than La Crescenta?

Over the twelve months from November 2025 through October 2026, La Cañada's median single family sale price of $2,412,500 was about 72 percent higher than La Crescenta's $1,400,000, and average price per square foot was about 22 percent higher.

### How fast do homes sell in La Cañada Flintridge?

Single family homes took 27 days to sell on average over the past twelve months, and about three-quarters closed within 30 days of listing. In October 2026 the average was 24 days and the median was 11.

### Are there many condos in La Cañada Flintridge?

No. Only 10 condos closed in La Cañada Flintridge over the past twelve months, at a median sale price of $1,399,500, compared with 53 condo sales in La Crescenta. There were 4 active condo listings in October 2026, up from 2 a year earlier.

## The TL;DR

October moved La Cañada Flintridge's single family market in the opposite direction from La Crescenta: supply doubled to four months, listings rose, and pending sales fell by a third from a year ago, although compared with September the market was steady. Monthly prices looked lower on paper, but the sample was only 15 sales, buyers who closed still paid about 105 percent of list price on average, and the trailing-year median barely moved. It's a market that gave buyers more choice without handing out discounts. Condos remain a small segment, with only ten closings all year.
