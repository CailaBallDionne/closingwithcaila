**La Cañada Flintridge's single family market cooled more noticeably in September than La Crescenta did: pending sales dropped 36 percent year over year and months of supply climbed a third, even as the trailing-year numbers show prices holding firm and homes actually selling a bit faster than they were a month ago.**

That's the headline. Here's what's underneath it.

## September Showed the Clearest Cooling Signal Yet

Closed single family sales in La Cañada came in at 11 in September 2026, down from 14 in September 2025, a 21 percent drop. Pending sales fell harder: 16 this year versus 25 last year, a 36 percent decline, the sharpest monthly pullback either city has shown all year. Months of supply rose to 4 from 3 a year ago, a 33 percent increase, tipping just slightly further toward balance. Active listings came in at 60, down 12 percent from 68 a year ago.

Now, before we all freak out, La Cañada sees roughly half the closed sales La Crescenta does in a given month, so swings like this show as bigger percentage moves on fewer actual transactions. One soft September isn't proof of a new direction, but it is the most pronounced single-month pullback in either market this year.

## The Trailing Year: Prices Holding, Even Picking Up Pace

The twelve-month rollup now covers October 2025 through September 2026, and it tells a steadier story than the September snapshot alone.

163 single family homes closed over the trailing year, for a combined $471,715,368 in total sold volume. Average sale price came in at $2,893,959, median at $2,445,090, both up modestly from where they stood a month ago. Average days to sell actually improved to 27 days, down from 29 last month, meaning homes are moving a bit faster despite September's softer pace. Homes sold for 101.54% of their most recent list price, and average price per square foot landed at $1,012.12, still roughly 23 percent higher than La Crescenta's $826.28.

Speed remains strong here too. 124 of the 163 sales, over three-quarters, closed within 30 days of hitting the market, an even higher share than La Crescenta's two-thirds. Only 5 sat for longer than 120 days.

## Condos: A Market That Barely Exists Here

La Cañada's condo segment is small enough that month-to-month swings don't mean much. Active condo listings held flat at 2 in both September 2025 and September 2026. Over the full trailing year, only 9 condos closed, for a combined $11,882,000, at an average price of $1,320,222 and a median of $1,400,000. Average days to sell ran 46 days, the longest of any segment covered in either city's report, and homes sold for 104.47% of list price, actually the highest sale-to-list ratio in this entire report, on a very small sample.

If condo inventory and selection matter to your search, La Crescenta is the better market to be looking in right now, its condo segment has far more active listings and a much deeper pool of recent sales. I cover that in detail in [this month's La Crescenta update](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-crescenta-market-update-september-2026).

## The Rental Market, Briefly

Rentals aren't my focus, but they're a useful read on underlying demand. In La Cañada, 97 leases closed over the trailing year at an average of $7,153 a month and a median of $6,500, nearly double La Crescenta's median. Landlords got 98.74% of asking rent, and leases took about 32 days to sign on average. Nothing alarming in either direction, demand here stays steady at a real premium over La Crescenta.

## What This Means If You're Buying

September's pullback, especially in pending sales, is the clearest sign yet that competition here has eased from where it stood earlier in the year. Months of supply at four is close to a balanced market rather than a strongly seller-favored one. That said, the trailing-year price data shows no softening, median and average prices are both up slightly from last month, so don't read this as a discount window. It's more room to negotiate and less pressure to rush, not a price drop.

Two practical things worth doing before you're seriously touring: get [actually pre-approved, not just pre-qualified](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/pre-approval-vs-pre-qualification), and budget for [what homeowners insurance costs here now](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/foothills-homeowners-insurance-after-eaton-fire), since it's a bigger line item than it used to be.

## What This Means If You're Selling

A sharper pullback in pending sales means fewer buyers actively writing offers right now than a year ago, and with months of supply creeping toward 4, pricing accuracy matters more than it did in the spring. The encouraging counterpoint: homes that are priced well are still selling fast, the trailing-year average days to sell actually improved this month. The speed gap between well-priced and overpriced listings is likely to widen from here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is La Cañada Flintridge's market cooling faster than La Crescenta's?

Based on September's numbers, yes. Pending sales fell 36 percent year over year in La Cañada versus 4 percent in La Crescenta, and months of supply rose a third versus holding flat. That said, La Cañada's trailing-year prices actually firmed slightly, so this reads as a demand pullback rather than a price correction so far.

### Is La Cañada Flintridge a buyer's or seller's market right now?

It's shifting toward balanced. Months of supply at 4 is close to the line most agents use to separate seller's and buyer's conditions (typically around 4 to 6 months), a change from the more clearly seller-favored 3 months of a year ago.

### How much more expensive is La Cañada Flintridge than La Crescenta?

Substantially. Over the trailing year, La Cañada's median single family sale price was $2,445,090 versus La Crescenta's $1,400,000, roughly 75 percent higher, and price per square foot ran about 23 percent higher.

### Are condos an option in La Cañada Flintridge?

Barely. Only 9 condos closed there over the full trailing year, compared to La Crescenta's much larger and faster-growing condo market. If condo inventory matters to your search, La Crescenta is the stronger option right now.

### How does renting in La Cañada Flintridge compare to La Crescenta?

More expensive. The median lease price in La Cañada over the trailing year was $6,500 a month, compared to $3,785 in La Crescenta, nearly double.

## The TL;DR

September was La Cañada Flintridge's clearest cooling signal of the year: pending sales down sharply, months of supply rising toward balanced territory, and fewer closed sales than a year ago. But the trailing-year fundamentals tell a calmer story, prices are steady to slightly up, and homes are actually selling a little faster than they were last month. This looks like softening demand rather than softening prices, at least for now. Condos remain a non-factor here, and rents held their usual steep premium over La Crescenta.
