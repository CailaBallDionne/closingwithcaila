If you've spent any time searching for a home in the Foothills, you've noticed that La Cañada Flintridge, La Crescenta, and Montrose tend to get mentioned in the same breath. They're neighbors, they share the same mountain backdrop, and they share a lot of the same buyers.

I live and work in La Crescenta, and spend a great deal of time in Montrose and La Cañada. The reality (from this realtor, anyway) is that we bop around between these sister neighborhoods. We're in the Crescenta Highlands, which is the most northwestern part of La Crescenta (so the furthest away from Montrose and La Cañada), and we still think of all of it as our extended neighborhood.

Closer to our home in La Crescenta, we can walk to [Deukmejian Wilderness Park](https://www.glendaleca.gov/government/departments/community-services-parks/parks-facilities-historic-sites/deukmejian-wilderness-park#ad-image-0) for hiking and views for days. We often have deer in our front yard, and have a bear that frequents our pool despite my "No Bears" sign. Jeremy's Coffee Shop is our go-to diner, and there are so many parks to choose from for playdates. That, and I will never get over how beautiful the late fall and winter sunsets are from La Crescenta.

La Cañada is the Posh Spice of the group, with pristinely landscaped yards, upscale restaurants like [Taylor's Steakhouse](https://taylorssteakhouse.com/), and the La Cañada Flintridge Country Club and golf course. For outdoor activities, La Cañada is home to Descanso Gardens and Flint Canyon Trail (the latter offers horseback riding). Not to be glossed over: La Cañada has the Target.

I consider Montrose to be the mutual "downtown" for La Crescenta and La Cañada. (And yes, you can tell I've adapted to suburban life after transitioning from Northeast LA when I'm calling Montrose "downtown.") We are downtown most weekends enjoying the pedestrian paradise of Montrose, usually spending too much money at [Current Season](https://shopcurrentseason.com/), perusing the shelves at [Once Upon a Time](https://shoponceuponatime.com/) or [Lost Books](https://www.lastbookstorela.com/montrose-ca), having a Billionaire cocktail at [Basin 141](https://www.basin141.com/), or getting ramen at [Kontentsu](https://www.kontentsula.com/). And if it's an occasion, we're enjoying a date night at [Backbone](https://www.backbonerestaurant.com/) or [Sushi Nakata](https://www.instagram.com/sushinakata_omakase/).

The Montrose Shopping Park runs along Honolulu Avenue, and there's a Sunday Harvest Market held there with farm vendors and live music. It's typically open Sundays 8 to 2, but check [their site](https://shopmontrose.com/harvest-market-montrose/) for updates before you plan a visit around it. I also love how Montrose shows off for the holidays. Come "downtown" any weekend between Thanksgiving and Christmas and you'll find anything from free photos with Santa, free horse-drawn carriage rides, and carolers (I'm telling you, it goes full Gilmore Girls over the holidays) to a holiday wine walk, a holiday parade where a helicopter flies low overhead with Santa waving from the door (sounds made up but isn't), and a full Christmas market.

That's how these three feel from the ground. They are also three distinct places, with different local governments, different school districts, and noticeably different price ranges, so I pulled the numbers and lined them up side by side. Now for the part with no adjectives: just what actually closed.

**Over the 12 months ending November 21, 2026, the median closed single-family sale price was about $2.42 million in La Cañada Flintridge (164 sales), $1.34 million in La Crescenta's ZIP code 91214 (199 sales), and $1.13 million in Montrose's ZIP code 91020 (16 sales, a small sample). Homes in all three typically sold in about three weeks or less. La Cañada Flintridge homes were the largest, sat on the biggest lots, and had the highest price per square foot.**

That's the headline. Here's everything underneath it.

## The Numbers Side by Side

All figures below are for single-family homes that closed between November 21, 2025 and November 21, 2026, pulled from the MLS. I'll explain exactly how I counted at the end of the post.

| Metric (past 12 months) | La Cañada Flintridge | La Crescenta (91214) | Montrose (91020) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closed sales | 164 | 199 | 16 |
| Median sale price | $2,420,055 | $1,340,000 | $1,130,000 |
| Median sale price per square foot | $1,015 | $794 | $878 |
| Median days on market | 16 | 17 | 21 |
| Median sale price vs. original list price | 101.1% | 100.9% | 100.0% |
| Share that sold above original list price | 57% | 55% | 47% |
| Median home size | 2,643 sq ft | 1,731 sq ft | 1,230 sq ft |
| Median lot size | 14,959 sq ft | 7,632 sq ft | 5,806 sq ft |

A few things jump out.

**The price gap is big, and it tracks home size.** La Cañada Flintridge's median sale price ran roughly 81 percent above La Crescenta's, and its median home was about 900 square feet larger on a lot roughly twice the size. Montrose's median price ran about 16 percent below La Crescenta's, with a median home about 500 square feet smaller on a smaller lot.

**Price per square foot doesn't line up the same way.** La Cañada Flintridge was highest at $1,015, which is about 28 percent above La Crescenta's $794. Montrose came in at $878, about 11 percent above La Crescenta, even though its total prices were lowest. Smaller homes often show a higher price per square foot because the land and the location make up a bigger share of what a buyer is paying for, so a number like this is worth reading alongside home size rather than on its own.

**Speed and competition looked similar.** The typical home in all three sold within about three weeks, and the typical sale landed within a point of the original list price. A little over half of the homes in La Cañada Flintridge and La Crescenta sold for more than their original list price, which is worth knowing if you're planning your offer strategy. (My post on [what to do when an appraisal comes in low](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/home-appraisal-comes-in-low-california-buyer-options) covers what that can mean for your financing.)

**A note on Montrose.** Sixteen sales in a full year is a small sample, and one or two unusual sales can move a median noticeably. Treat the Montrose figures as a picture of the last year's sales, not a trend. There were only 3 Montrose sales in the most recent three months, which is too few to say anything meaningful, so I've left Montrose out of the recent-trend comparison below.

## What the Most Recent Three Months Look Like

For the three months ending November 21, 2026, I compared the two areas with enough sales to measure.

La Cañada Flintridge had 42 closed sales, a median sale price of $2,325,000, a median of 13 days on market, and about 66 percent of homes selling above original list price. La Crescenta had 48 closed sales, a median sale price of $1,307,500, a median of 23 days on market, and about 43 percent selling above original list price.

In other words, over the last few months La Cañada Flintridge sales moved faster and more often above asking than La Crescenta's did, compared with a 12-month picture where the two looked close. That's one quarter of data, so I wouldn't build a prediction on it, but it's the kind of detail that's useful to know before you write an offer in either place. My monthly updates for [La Crescenta](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-crescenta-market-update-september-2026) and [La Cañada Flintridge](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-canada-flintridge-market-update-september-2026) go deeper on supply, pending sales, and month-over-month movement.

If your budget is under $1.5 million, the data says your realistic options in these three places sit mostly in La Crescenta and Montrose. If your budget is over $2 million, La Cañada Flintridge is where most of the sales happened, and about 20 percent of its sales over the year (33 homes) closed above $4 million. The $1.5 to $2 million range is the one place where all three overlap, but even there La Crescenta and La Cañada Flintridge did most of the volume.

## How These Three Places Are Actually Organized

The names get used loosely, so here's what each one is.

**La Cañada Flintridge** is an incorporated city, [formed in 1976 when the cities of Flintridge and La Cañada merged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ca%C3%B1ada_Flintridge,_California). It has its own city government.

**La Crescenta and Montrose** are two parts of one community, La Crescenta-Montrose, which is [an unincorporated area of Los Angeles County](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1195186_UAAnnualReport-LaCrescenta-MontroseFY23-24.pdf) rather than a city. The LA County Board of Supervisors fills the role a city council would. The community's two main ZIP codes are [91214 for La Crescenta and 91020 for Montrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crescenta-Montrose,_California). One wrinkle: [some areas that go by the name Montrose sit inside the City of Glendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescenta_Valley), so confirm jurisdiction by address rather than by the name on the listing.

That's the reason I split the data by ZIP code. In the MLS, some homes in La Crescenta are listed with Glendale as the city. Of the 199 sales in ZIP 91214, 42 carried a Glendale city label, so searching or filtering by city name alone would have left out a meaningful slice of La Crescenta. (La Cañada Flintridge's figures are filtered by city name, since that's an actual city.)

Why does city versus unincorporated matter to a buyer? Mostly because it determines which agency handles things like permits for an addition or ADU. That varies by address, so it's worth confirming for the specific property you're looking at. If you're thinking about adding a unit down the line, my post on [whether to build an ADU before selling](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/should-you-build-adu-before-selling-california) is a good place to start.

## School Districts: The Boundary Isn't the City Line

[Glendale Unified serves the city of Glendale, portions of La Cañada Flintridge, and the unincorporated communities of Montrose and La Crescenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale_Unified_School_District). Crescenta Valley High School, for example, is a Glendale Unified school located in La Crescenta. [La Cañada Unified covers the majority of La Cañada Flintridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ca%C3%B1ada_Unified_School_District) and runs three elementary schools plus a combined junior and senior high school.

So a La Cañada Flintridge address is not automatically a La Cañada Unified address. A part of the far west side of the city known as Sagebrush, [west of Rosebank Drive out to Pickens Canyon](https://www.crescentavalleyweekly.com/?p=136858), was historically inside Glendale Unified boundaries even though it carries a La Cañada Flintridge address. However, Sagebrush voters approved a measure to move that territory into La Cañada Unified in November 2024, which was implemented in October 2026.

How to check any address:

- For Glendale Unified, enter the street name in the district's [School Finder](https://gusd.net/schoolfinder). The district notes that results are estimates and gives a Student Support Services number, (818) 241-3111 ext. 1283, to confirm a school of residence.
- For La Cañada Unified, contact the district directly at [lcusd.net](https://www.lcusd.net) to confirm boundaries for a specific address.

Schools are the part of this comparison where it helps to use the same yardstick for both districts. The most consistent one is California's own standardized test, the Smarter Balanced assessment, which every district takes. In spring 2026, [88.14 percent of La Cañada Unified students met or exceeded the state standard in English and 84.31 percent in math](https://caaspp.edsource.org/sbac/la-canada-unified-19646590000000), compared with [60.7 percent in English and 54.64 percent in math across Glendale Unified](https://caaspp.edsource.org/sbac/glendale-unified-19645680000000) and [49.27 percent and 38.35 percent statewide](https://caaspp.edsource.org/sbac/california-00000000000000). Glendale Unified's figure covers schools across the city of Glendale as well as La Crescenta and Montrose, so the results for a specific school are a better comparison for any one address. The [California School Dashboard comparison tool](https://edsource.org/2025/search-and-compare-data-from-the-california-school-dashboard-2025/744874) shows test scores alongside graduation, attendance, and other measures for individual schools. Awards and third-party grades, like National Blue Ribbon recognition or Niche rankings, are worth a glance, but they come from different years and use different criteria, so they don't line up the way test results do. The best way to evaluate any school is still to visit it and talk to the people who work there.

## A Few Things the Sales Data Can't Show You

**Lot size shapes how a place feels.** With a median lot of roughly 15,000 square feet, La Cañada Flintridge homes typically have more room around them than the roughly 7,600 square feet typical in La Crescenta or 5,800 in Montrose. Whether that's a plus depends on what you want to do with the space, and what you want to maintain.

**They all share foothill living.** That means views and trails, and it also means paying attention to wildfire risk, insurance availability, and cost before you fall for a house. I wrote about that in [homeowners insurance in the Foothills after the Eaton Fire](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/foothills-homeowners-insurance-after-eaton-fire).

**Commute is personal.** The honest answer is to drive your real route at your real hours before you decide. A map won't tell you what a Tuesday at 8 a.m. feels like.

## Which One Fits What You're Looking For?

I don't think there's a "best" one, but the data does make a few things clearer.

**Start with the budget.** Under $1.5 million, most of the activity was in La Crescenta and Montrose. Between $1.5 and $2 million, La Crescenta and La Cañada Flintridge both had real volume. Over $2 million, it was overwhelmingly La Cañada Flintridge.

**Then look at what you need the property to do.** If you want a larger home or a bigger lot, the median numbers point to La Cañada Flintridge. If you want a smaller home with a central shopping district nearby, Montrose is worth a look. La Crescenta sits in the middle on size and has the most sales volume of the three, which usually means more choices to compare.

**Then check the schools by address, not by town name,** for the reasons above.

## Where This Data Comes From

Because I'd rather you know exactly what's behind the numbers:

- **Source and dates:** MLS closed sales for single-family homes between November 21, 2025 and November 21, 2026, pulled on November 21, 2026. The MLS data is deemed reliable but not guaranteed.
- **Geography:** La Crescenta and Montrose are filtered by ZIP code (91214 and 91020). La Cañada Flintridge is filtered by city.
- **Medians, not averages,** so a handful of very expensive sales don't distort the picture. The one exception is that I cite the share of sales above original list price as a percentage of the sales that had an original list price on record.
- **Days on market** excludes sales recorded with zero days on market, which tend to be off-market or private transactions rather than typical listings.
- **New construction:** Three of the La Crescenta sales were newly built homes (2024 through 2026). Excluding them doesn't change the median sale price.
- **One-off sales stay in.** I didn't remove unusual sales, such as a home that closed well above its list price, because they're part of what happened.
- **Why these numbers won't match my monthly market updates:** those reports use a broader MLS area for La Crescenta that also includes parts of Glendale, and they cover different date ranges. This post is built on ZIP codes and a single 12-month window so the three areas line up with each other.
- **Montrose** is a small sample at 16 sales, and its figures should be read with that in mind.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is La Cañada Flintridge more expensive than La Crescenta?

Yes. For single-family homes that closed in the 12 months ending November 21, 2026, La Cañada Flintridge's median sale price was about $2.42 million, compared with about $1.34 million in La Crescenta's ZIP code 91214. The median home in La Cañada Flintridge was also larger and sat on a bigger lot.

### What is the difference between La Crescenta and Montrose?

They're two parts of the same unincorporated Los Angeles County community, La Crescenta-Montrose, with different ZIP codes: 91214 for La Crescenta and 91020 for Montrose. In the past 12 months, Montrose's median sale price was about $1.13 million on 16 sales, compared with about $1.34 million on 199 sales in La Crescenta, and Montrose homes were typically smaller.

### Is La Crescenta part of Glendale?

No. La Crescenta is an unincorporated community of Los Angeles County, not part of the city of Glendale. Some La Crescenta homes are listed in the MLS with Glendale as the city, and the area's public schools are run by Glendale Unified, but the community itself is not within Glendale's city limits.

### Which school district is La Cañada Flintridge in?

Most of La Cañada Flintridge is in La Cañada Unified School District, but the city also includes areas served by Glendale Unified. A section on the far west side known as Sagebrush was historically in Glendale Unified, and Sagebrush voters approved a transfer to La Cañada Unified in November 2024. Confirm the school district for any specific address directly with the districts.

### How fast do homes sell in La Cañada Flintridge, La Crescenta, and Montrose?

Based on the 12 months ending November 21, 2026, the median home spent about 16 days on market in La Cañada Flintridge, 17 in La Crescenta, and 21 in Montrose. Over the most recent three months, the median was 13 days in La Cañada Flintridge and 23 days in La Crescenta.

## The TL;DR

La Cañada Flintridge, La Crescenta, and Montrose are neighbors, but they're three different markets. La Cañada Flintridge has the largest homes, the biggest lots, and the highest prices of the three. La Crescenta is the biggest in volume, and the one where most sales landed between $1 million and $2 million. Montrose is the smallest sample, with smaller homes and a central shopping district. School districts and local government don't follow the same lines as the city names, so confirm those by address.
