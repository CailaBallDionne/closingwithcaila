Not to bring everything back to my true passion of reality television, but every house hunting, sunset selling, luxury listing show I have ever watched ends the same way: After a fierce negotiation, the offer is accepted and the credits roll. Which is a pretty bold place to stop, when you think about it, because "accepted" is where most of the actual home buying process begins.

Between that moment and the day you get your keys is a stretch of deadlines, signatures, and decisions, and a lot of it is handled by people you have only just met: an escrow officer, a lender, an appraiser, an inspector.

For most people, a home purchase is their largest financial transaction, so this 30-ish day period of basically holding your breath can be stressful (understatement). The mountains of dense paperwork don't help, either. So while I can't take away the purgatory-esque vibes, I can at least demystify the process so you know what to expect when you're expecting...keys.

**Once your offer is accepted in California, escrow opens and a set of contract deadlines starts counting: your earnest money deposit is typically due within three business days, inspections and contingency decisions typically land around day 17, and closing happens on the date your contract sets, after your loan is final and you have completed a final walk-through.**

Here is the full timeline in order, so none of it catches you off guard.

## First, How the Clock Works

Escrow is a neutral third party, usually an escrow or title company, that holds the money and paperwork until everyone has done what the contract says. Most deadlines in a California purchase agreement are counted in calendar days from the day your offer is accepted, and the acceptance day itself counts as day zero.

The numbers in this post are standard default timeframes. If you are working with an agent, your contract and your agent are the final word on your actual dates. If you are still researching, this is what a typical timeline looks like:

- **Day 0:** Offer accepted, escrow opens
- **Within 3 business days:** Earnest money deposit goes into escrow
- **Within 7 days:** Seller disclosures arrive
- **Days 1 to 17:** Inspections, title review, and appraisal, with contingency decisions around day 17
- **Mid-escrow:** Homeowners insurance and final loan approval
- **At least 3 business days before closing:** Closing Disclosure
- **Within 5 days before closing:** Final walk-through
- **Closing:** Sign, fund, record, and get your keys

## Day 0: Escrow Opens

Once both sides have signed the Residential Purchase Agreement (RPA), an escrow officer is assigned and you will get an introduction with their contact information and instructions. Save it somewhere easy to find.

One habit worth building right away: if an email ever arrives with new or changed wiring instructions, call your escrow officer at the phone number from that original introduction before sending a dollar. Wire fraud aimed at homebuyers is a real thing, and a quick phone call is cheap insurance.

## Within 3 Business Days: Your Earnest Money Deposit

Your earnest money deposit is the good-faith money that shows the seller you are serious, typically [1 to 3 percent of the purchase price](https://baylegal.com/?p=23078). It goes into escrow, not to the seller, and under the standard contract it is typically due within three business days of acceptance.

Ask your lender how they want the deposit documented, especially if any of it came from a family member. If that applies to you, my post on [down payment gift funds](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/down-payment-gift-funds-california) covers what to have ready.

## Within 7 Days: Seller Disclosures Arrive

The standard California purchase agreement form gives the seller seven days after acceptance to deliver their disclosures, such as the Transfer Disclosure Statement and the Seller Property Questionnaire.

Read every page. This is where the seller shares what they know about the home's condition, and it should shape what you ask your inspector to look at closely.

(Psst: If you want a deep dive on this, I've got you covered with my [guide to California seller disclosures](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/california-seller-disclosures-tds-spq-avid-nhd).)

## Days 1 to 17: Inspections, Title, and the Appraisal

This is the busiest stretch, and it runs on your contingencies. [In California, the default period to investigate the property is 17 days](https://garmolaw.com/what-contingencies-should-be-in-your-california-real-estate-contract/). Here is what typically happens inside that window:

**Inspections.** A licensed inspector goes through the home top to bottom. Depending on the property, you may also bring in specialists, such as a roofer, a sewer line inspector, or a foundation expert. Once you have the reports, you can generally ask for repairs or a credit, move forward as is, or cancel under your inspection contingency and (crucially) receive your earnest money deposit back.

**Title review.** The title company issues a preliminary title report showing what is attached to the property, like liens and easements. Read it, and ask questions about anything that looks unfamiliar.

**The appraisal.** Your lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home is worth what you agreed to pay. If it comes in low, you have options, and I break those down in my post on [real estate contingencies](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/what-are-real-estate-contingencies).

**HOA documents.** If the home is [in an HOA](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/buying-a-condo-los-angeles-hoa-guide), you will receive the CC&Rs, financials, and meeting minutes to review.

The appraisal and loan contingencies run on their own deadlines, so confirm each date in your contract rather than assuming they all land on the same day.

## Around Day 17: Removing Your Contingencies

This step surprises a lot of first-time buyers. California uses what is called active removal: a contingency stays in effect until you remove it in writing, rather than simply expiring when the clock runs out.

That works in your favor, and it also means removing a contingency is a deliberate step you sign off on, not something that happens by default. Once you remove one, you have given up the protection it provided, and your deposit is more exposed if you decide to back out.

Before you remove your loan contingency in particular, get your lender's go-ahead. Your lender is the right person to tell you where your file stands.

## Mid-Escrow: Insurance and Final Loan Approval

Two things are running quietly in the background while inspections and contingencies get the attention (well, three things, if you count your real estate agent's coffee maker).

**Homeowners insurance.** Lenders generally require proof of homeowners insurance before closing, and it can take some time to line up, so start early rather than in the final week. Especially if you are looking in an area where [securing cost-effective insurance](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/foothills-homeowners-insurance-after-eaton-fire) may require multiple bids. Ask your lender what proof they need and by when.

**Final loan approval.** Your lender is working toward final approval, often called "clear to close," from the file that began with your [pre-approval](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/pre-approval-vs-pre-qualification). Final approval typically includes verifying your employment again shortly before closing. Lenders also commonly ask buyers to hold off on new debt, large purchases, and job changes until the loan funds. What applies to your loan is a question for your lender, so ask them directly and early.

## At Least 3 Business Days Before Closing: The Closing Disclosure

[Federal rules require your lender to make sure you receive your Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing](https://consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-do-if-i-do-not-get-a-closing-disclosure-three-days-before-my-mortgage-closing-en-1911). It lists the final terms and costs of your loan. Compare it line by line to your original Loan Estimate, and send any questions to your lender right away.

For this rule, [business days include Saturdays](https://www.alta.org/blog/post/how-to-comply-with-the-closing-disclosures-three-day-rule).

## Within 5 Days Before Closing: The Final Walk-Through

[The standard contract calls for a final verification of the property's condition within five days before close of escrow](https://legaltemplates.net/wp-content/uploads/California-Residential-Purchase-Agreement-and-Joint-Escrow-Instructions-Form-RPA.pdf). This is your last look before closing. You are confirming that the home is in the condition you expect, that any agreed repairs were completed, and that nothing is missing that should stay.

If something is off, tell your agent right away, while there is still time to resolve it before closing.

## Closing: Sign, Fund, Record, Keys

Near the end, you will sign your loan documents and escrow's closing paperwork, often with escrow or a mobile notary. You will also send any remaining funds to escrow, which will tell you the exact amount. As with your deposit, only wire to instructions you have verified by phone.

Once everything is signed and the funds are in, escrow records the deed with the county and the sale is official. Whether you get your keys that same day depends on the possession terms in your contract. If the seller is staying for a short period after closing, my post on [rent-back agreements](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/rent-back-agreement-selling-before-buying-california) explains how that works.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does escrow take in California?

The closing date is set in your purchase agreement, either as a specific date or as a number of days after acceptance. The right timeline depends on your contract, your loan, and the seller's situation, so your agent and lender can give you a realistic date before you make an offer.

### When is my earnest money deposit due?

Under the standard California purchase agreement, the deposit is typically due within three business days after your offer is accepted. Your contract will state the exact deadline.

### Can I back out after my offer is accepted?

Yes, but how and when matters. While a contingency is still in effect, you can generally cancel under that contingency and protect your deposit. In California, a contingency stays in effect until you remove it in writing, and after that, backing out can put your earnest money deposit at risk. Talk to your agent before you remove any contingency, and ask a real estate attorney about any specific legal question.

### What is the final walk-through?

The final walk-through, called the final verification of condition in the California purchase agreement, is a check of the home shortly before closing, typically within five days, to confirm its condition and that any agreed repairs were completed.

## The Bottom Line

Escrow can feel like a month of waiting broken up by urgent emails. In reality it is a short list of dates, and each one has a purpose: protecting your deposit, giving you time to learn about the home, and giving your lender time to finish the loan. Once you know what is coming, it is mostly paperwork instead of surprises.
