After two months of cooler numbers in the La Crescenta housing market, October did something different. Thankfully, not in a spooky way, despite the Halloween influence. If you've been reading along, the last two updates ([September](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-crescenta-market-update-september-2026) and [August](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-crescenta-market-update-august-2026)) were all about the market softening. This month the single family side tightened back up, and the condo side was a whole different animal.

So let's get into it, and look at the La Crescenta housing market in October 2025 compared to October 2026, plus the trailing twelve months of MLS data, for single family homes and condos. La Cañada Flintridge gets its own post again because Beverly Foothills deserves her moment in the spotlight, and [that update lives here](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/la-canada-flintridge-market-update-october-2026).

A quick note on the data: these numbers come from MLS reports I pulled on November 6, 2026, and La Crescenta here includes the Montrose and Glendale annex areas, same as always. MLS counts get revised as late-reported sales come in, so every comparison below uses the current figures.

**La Crescenta's single family market tightened in October: active listings fell 24 percent from a year ago, months of supply dropped from four to two, and pending sales rose 22 percent, but the typical home sold right at asking price instead of above it, and the median sale price held flat.**

That's the headline. Here's what's underneath it.

## October Flipped the Script

Closed single family sales came in at 19 in October 2026, down from 20 in October 2025, a 5 percent dip. Pending sales, which tell you what's about to close, rose to 28 from 23, up 22 percent. After two months where pending sales ran below last year, that's a nice little change in direction.

The inventory side moved even more. Active listings fell from 82 to 62, down 24 percent, and months of supply (how long the current inventory would take to sell at the recent pace) dropped from 4 to 2. Anything under three months generally favors sellers. New listings, the homes that actually came to market during the month, fell to 18 from 32, a 44 percent drop.

Month over month, the picture is the same, just sharper. Compared with September, pending sales rose to 28 from 22, closed sales ticked up to 19 from 18, active listings dropped to 62 from 82, and months of supply fell to 2 from 4. (September's 82 active listings happen to match October 2025's 82, so the drop is 24 percent both ways. Coincidence, not a typo.) New listings and average days on market also fell from September.

Put those together: fewer homes came to market than last October, more of them went under contract, and what's left on the shelf shrank. While the data shows what happened, not why, my read is that fewer sellers listed this October and buyers worked through what was available. Whether that holds through the holiday months is something I'll be watching in the next update.

## Prices Held Steady, but Not Everything Moved Together

The median sale price in October was $1,300,000, versus $1,305,550 a year ago, essentially flat. The average sale price rose 7 percent, to $1,391,830 from $1,298,155. When the average climbs and the median doesn't, it usually means a few higher-priced sales pulled the average up while the typical sale stayed put.

Also worth knowing: October's median of $1.3 million sits below the $1.4 million trailing-year median. One month with 19 sales can wander, which is why I lean on the twelve-month numbers below.

The number I'd pay the most attention to this month is the median sale-to-list ratio, which shows what a typical sale looks like instead of letting one or two outliers skew it. The typical La Crescenta home sold for 100 percent of its last list price in October, versus 103 percent a year ago. In other words, the typical home sold at asking this October, where last year it sold a few points over. Competition picked up, but not every offer is winning by overbidding.

Days on market tells a split story. The average days on market fell to 20 from 29, down 31 percent, while the median stayed at 16 days both years. The typical listing took about the same two-plus weeks it did last year, and the average dropped most likely because fewer homes lingered on the market.

## The Trailing Year: Steady as She Goes

The twelve-month rollup now covers November 2025 through October 2026, and it barely moved from last month, which is the point. The closed count held at 231, the median stayed at $1,400,000, average days to sell stayed at 28, and the average sale price edged up from $1,430,617 to $1,438,546. This market isn't swinging in this neck of the woods, it's leveling off.

231 single family homes closed over the trailing year, for a combined $332,304,230 in total sold volume. The average sale price came in at $1,438,546 and the median at $1,400,000, close enough together that the median is a fair picture of a typical sale. Homes took 28 days to sell on average, sale prices averaged 102.81 percent of list prices, and average price per square foot landed at $826.58.

The $1,000,001 to $2,000,000 range is still where the action is: 179 of the 231 sales, about 77 percent, landed there, with three and four bedroom homes making up most of it. Another 37 homes sold for under $1 million, and 15 sold above $2 million.

Speed is still the defining feature. 153 of the 231 sales, about two-thirds, closed within 30 days of hitting the market, and only 6 sat longer than 120 days. That pace is exactly why I keep saying to get [pre-approved, not just pre-qualified](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/pre-approval-vs-pre-qualification) before you're seriously touring.

## Condos: Inventory Is Easing, Supply Is Not

The condo story this year has been inventory, so let's start there. Active condo listings in La Crescenta were 20 in October 2026, down from 22 in September and up 33 percent from 15 a year ago. That's a much smaller gap than September's 22 versus 8. Condo inventory built from 9 listings in February to a summer high of around 35, and it has come down every month since.

Sales haven't kept pace with the listings that remain. Five condos closed in October, the same as October 2025 but well below a busy September, and pending condo sales fell to 2 from 4. With 20 active listings and only a handful of sales a month, the MLS shows condo months of supply at roughly 10, versus about 4 both a year ago and in September. Condo numbers are small, so single months swing, but that's a very different picture from the two months of supply on the single family side.

Over the trailing twelve months, 53 condos closed for a combined $41,187,716. The average sale price was $777,127 and the median was $800,000. Condos took 32 days to sell on average, sale prices averaged 100.29 percent of list prices, and price per square foot averaged $569.52. The median barely moved from last month's $801,125, while the average slipped from $792,398. Twenty-seven of the 53 sold for under $800,000, and only 5 crossed $1 million. About half, 28 of the 53, closed within 30 days.

Compared with single family homes, the median condo price was about 43 percent lower ($800,000 versus $1,400,000), and price per square foot was about 31 percent lower. If you've been priced out of a house in the Foothills, it's worth considering if a condo could be your ticket to homeownership here (though factor in HOA costs to your monthly payment before making that assumption). More supply relative to sales generally gives a buyer more room to negotiate, though every building is different, and with condos the HOA paperwork matters as much as the unit. My [guide to buying a condo in Los Angeles](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/buying-a-condo-los-angeles-hoa-guide) covers what to read before you commit.

## What This Means If You're Buying

On the house side, the picture got a little more competitive in October: fewer new listings, more pending sales, and two months of supply. But the typical home sold at asking price, so there's no blanket rule that you need to overbid. What the right offer looks like depends on the specific house, how long it's been listed, and what the comparable sales show. And if you're budgeting for the full cost of owning here, it's worth knowing [what homeowners insurance costs in the Foothills now](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/foothills-homeowners-insurance-after-eaton-fire).

On the condo side, the numbers lean your way a bit more than they have in a while: more listings than a year ago, more months of supply, and a median price well below the single family median. Take your time with the HOA documents.

## What This Means If You're Selling

Fewer homes are competing with yours than a year ago: active listings are down 24 percent and new listings are down 44 percent. That's the good news. The cautionary part is that the typical home sold at asking, not above it, so pricing accurately matters more than hoping for a bidding war. A median of 16 days on market tells you well-priced homes are still moving quickly. Getting your [seller disclosures](https://closingwithcaila.com/blog/california-seller-disclosures-tds-spq-avid-nhd) organized before you list is one way to avoid slowing down once an offer comes in.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is La Crescenta a buyer's or seller's market right now?

Based on October 2026 MLS data, La Crescenta single family homes lean toward sellers, with months of supply dropping from 4 a year ago to 2. Anything under three months is generally considered a seller's market. That said, the typical home sold for right around its list price, not above it.

### How many homes sold in La Crescenta over the past year, and for how much?

Over the twelve months from November 2025 through October 2026, 231 single family homes closed in La Crescenta. The median sale price was $1,400,000 and the average was $1,438,546.

### Did home prices fall in La Crescenta in October?

Not meaningfully. The October median sale price was $1,300,000 versus $1,305,550 a year earlier, essentially flat, and the average sale price rose about 7 percent. The trailing twelve-month median held at $1,400,000.

### Are condos cheaper than houses in La Crescenta?

Yes. Over the trailing twelve months, the median condo sale price was $800,000 versus $1,400,000 for single family homes, about 43 percent lower, and condo price per square foot was about 31 percent lower.

### How long does it take to sell a home in La Crescenta?

Single family homes took 28 days to sell on average over the past twelve months, and about two-thirds closed within 30 days of listing. In October 2026, the average was 20 days and the median was 16.

## The TL;DR

After two softer months, October pulled La Crescenta's single family market back toward the seller's side: fewer new listings, shrinking inventory, more pending sales, and two months of supply. Month over month, pending sales rose from 22 to 28 and active listings fell from 82 to 62. But prices didn't jump, and the typical home sold right at asking. The trailing-year numbers, the ones that describe the market over time, barely moved. Condos are the opposite story: inventory is easing from its summer high, but supply is still well above where it was a year ago, with a median price far below the single family median.
